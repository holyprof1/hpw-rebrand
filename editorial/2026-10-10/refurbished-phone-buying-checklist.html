The cheapest refurbished phone is only a good buy if it can do what you need and you have a workable route back to the seller when something is wrong. Compare the exact listing rather than assuming every device carrying the word refurbished has the same standard.

For the marketplace model itself, start with our Back Market explainer. This article is a buying checklist, not a review of a handset we purchased.

Separate appearance from battery condition

A neat-looking phone and a healthy battery answer different questions. Ask what minimum battery capacity is promised and whether the listing offers a replacement battery. Do not interpret an appearance grade as a battery test result unless the seller explicitly connects them.

For example, Back Market's US Premium-device policy describes a minimum 90% battery capacity and genuine manufacturer parts for that specific tier. That is not a promise covering every seller's refurbished phone or every grade. Save the listing you actually order.

Match the exact model to your network

Write down the model identifier, storage size and whether it is unlocked. If you plan to use it in Nigeria or another country, check the exact variant with your mobile network rather than relying on the broad product name. Ask whether it uses a physical SIM, eSIM or both. A lower price is less useful if your intended connection will not work.

Confirm locks and ownership before payment

Ask the seller to confirm that the phone will arrive ready for your own account, without an old owner's activation lock or an organisation's management restriction. If a private seller cannot demonstrate this, pause the transaction. Make the requirement explicit in writing so there is a clear record of what was promised.

Check what is included

Budget for accessories as part of the total cost. Back Market's accessory guidance says smartphone orders include a compatible charging cable, while wall chargers and earphones are not included. Other sellers can have different bundles. Check the product page instead of assuming a sealed-box experience.

Read the return route as carefully as the warranty

A warranty period alone does not tell you who pays shipping or how a claim is handled. Record the seller, support channel, return window, exclusions and where the phone must be sent. The US Premium page states a 30-day change-of-mind policy and a seller's one-year limited warranty. Those are scoped US terms; check the country and current listing before relying on them.

Keep a short arrival checklist

  1. Photograph the packaging and compare the model and condition with your saved order.
  2. Set up your own account and test your intended SIM or connection.
  3. Check charging, cameras, speakers, microphone, screen and buttons.
  4. Record any fault promptly using the seller's stated support process.

This is a practical inspection plan, not proof of laboratory testing or a guarantee against hidden faults. If your priority is repair access rather than the lowest purchase price, our Fairphone article explains another approach to phone ownership.
