A VPN and a data-removal service address different parts of your online exposure. A VPN can protect the connection between your device and its server. A removal service tries to get personal records removed from the organisations it covers. Buying one does not automatically give you the other.

Our Incogni explainer describes the removal-service model. Our Surfshark VPN article covers the connection tool. This comparison helps you decide which question you need answered first.

What a VPN changes

Surfshark's encryption explanation describes an encrypted connection between your device and a VPN server. That addresses traffic on that connection. It does not itself send deletion requests to a people-search site that already lists your address.

Consider an everyday example: you turn on a VPN, then visit a site where you are signed into your own account. Changing the route of the connection does not turn that account into an unknown person. Treat claims of complete anonymity cautiously and ask which part of the system a tool actually changes.

What data removal targets

Incogni's opt-out guides describe identifying brokers, following each site's removal process and checking again because records may return. This targets stored information at particular organisations. It does not replace connection encryption.

Manual removal can be a starting point when you know which listing concerns you. A paid service may help with repeated requests, but the covered brokers, supported regions and current subscription terms matter more than a broad claim to remove you from the internet.

Use an exposure list before choosing a subscription

Make a private list of what you want to address: a visible people-search record, an old public profile, recurring messages, or concern about a network connection. Do not publish that list. Identify the organisation holding the record and the official support or removal route. If the problem is a public profile you control, start with its own visibility and account settings.

Questions to ask a removal provider

  • Does it cover the organisation that holds the record?
  • Does it support where you live?
  • What personal details must you provide to identify the record?
  • How does it show completed requests and unsuccessful ones?
  • Does it recheck, and what continues after cancellation?

These are comparison questions, not a finding that every provider supplies all those features. Ask for the current written terms.

A simple comparison

If your question is “how does this connection travel?”, investigate the VPN's scope. If it is “why is this broker listing my details?”, investigate the holder's removal process. If it is “why am I receiving suspicious messages?”, also check account security and the original source of the messages; neither product should be assumed to solve that entire problem.
